        **Research interests:** quantum field theory, particle physics, astrophysics, and physics beyond the Standard Model.

        The Standard Model of particle physics has achieved extraordinary success, but it leaves many fundamental questions unanswered, from the nature of dark matter to the origin of the matter–antimatter asymmetry. My research in particle theory and phenomenology explores such new physics using tools from quantum field theory (QFT) and effective field theory (EFT), with particular interest in connecting theoretical models to astrophysical and cosmological phenomena.

        My current research focuses on non-topological solitons, new particles carrying baryon number, and neutron dark decay. More broadly, I am interested in the rich phenomenology of scalar fields beyond the Standard Model, from nonperturbative field configurations to their implications for compact astrophysical objects. I study how nonlinear scalar interactions and gauge dynamics give rise to localized configurations such as Q-balls, as well as how new scalar degrees of freedom and their self-interactions can modify the properties of dense matter and neutron stars. My recent work includes studies of gauged and Proca Q-balls in flat scalar potentials and of scalar baryons produced in neutron-star matter.

        My previous research explored long-baseline atom interferometry as a precision probe of new physics, particularly low-frequency gravitational waves and ultralight dark matter. Using matter-wave interference, atom interferometers can probe gravitational-wave frequency bands complementary to those accessible to conventional laser interferometers, opening a new window onto astrophysical and cosmological sources and offering capabilities such as source localization. They can also search for ultralight dark matter through coherent, time-dependent effects induced by oscillating dark-matter fields on atomic systems. I am a member of the AION (Atom Interferometer Observatory and Network) Collaboration, which is developing atom-interferometric techniques for gravitational-wave detection and searches for new fundamental physics.
